For three centuries one dynasty ruled much of the subcontinent, leaving behind great cities, monuments, paintings and poetry. This is the story of the Mughals.

The Beginning

1526

Babur and the First Battle of Panipat

Babur, a Timurid prince from Central Asia, marched into northern India and faced Ibrahim Lodi, the Sultan of Delhi, at Panipat. Field artillery and disciplined tactics won the day against a much larger army.

1526Year of the battle
BaburFounder of the dynasty
PanipatEnd of the Delhi Sultanate

With Lodi defeated, Babur took Delhi and Agra. The Mughal Empire had begun.

The Mughal Timeline

Select a ruler to explore

Babur · 1526–1530

Won the First Battle of Panipat and founded the empire. He wrote the Baburnama, his memoir of campaigns, gardens and daily life.

Humayun · 1530–1556

Lost his throne to Sher Shah Suri, spent years in exile in Persia, then recovered Delhi in 1555. His tomb in Delhi became a model for later Mughal architecture.

Akbar · 1556–1605

Expanded and organised the empire, built Fatehpur Sikri, and followed a policy of tolerance towards different faiths.

Jahangir · 1605–1627

A great patron of painting and the study of nature. Empress Nur Jahan held major influence at court.

Shah Jahan · 1628–1658

The great builder. The Taj Mahal, the Red Fort and the Jama Masjid of Delhi all date from his reign.

Aurangzeb · 1658–1707

Ruled the largest territory in Mughal history, but long wars in the Deccan drained the treasury and stretched the army.

Later Mughals · 1707–1857

Weak emperors, regional rivals and foreign invasions reduced Mughal power. The last emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was exiled after the 1857 uprising.

The Great Emperors

Five rulers who shaped the empire

Babur

Reign: 1526 – 1530
Major developments
  • Founded the Mughal dynasty
  • Introduced gunpowder artillery tactics
  • Laid out formal Persian-style gardens
Important events
  • First Battle of Panipat, 1526
  • Battle of Khanwa against Rana Sanga, 1527
  • Wrote the Baburnama

Akbar

Reign: 1556 – 1605
Major developments
  • Mansabdari system of administration
  • Land revenue reforms
  • Policy of religious tolerance (Sulh-i-Kul)
Important events
  • Second Battle of Panipat, 1556
  • Founded Fatehpur Sikri, 1571
  • Conquest of Gujarat and Bengal

Jahangir

Reign: 1605 – 1627
Major developments
  • Peak of Mughal miniature painting
  • Study of birds, animals and plants in art
  • Shalimar and Nishat gardens in Kashmir
Important events
  • Marriage to Nur Jahan, 1611
  • Arrival of English envoy Sir Thomas Roe, 1615
  • Rebellion of Prince Khurram (Shah Jahan)

Shah Jahan

Reign: 1628 – 1658
Major developments
  • Golden age of Mughal architecture
  • Founded the walled city of Shahjahanabad
  • Peacock Throne
Important events
  • Death of Mumtaz Mahal, 1631
  • Taj Mahal built, 1632–1653
  • Deposed by his son Aurangzeb, 1658

Aurangzeb

Reign: 1658 – 1707
Major developments
  • Greatest territorial extent of the empire
  • Compilation of the Fatawa-i-Alamgiri
  • Badshahi Mosque built at Lahore
Important events
  • War of succession, 1657–1659
  • Long Deccan campaigns from 1681
  • Conflicts with the Marathas and Sikhs

Akbar's India

How Akbar shaped the empire

Administration

The mansabdari system ranked officers by number of troops and status. Todar Mal's land revenue system fixed taxes on measured land.

Architecture

Fatehpur Sikri and Agra Fort blended Persian design with Hindu and Jain traditions in red sandstone.

Religious policies

Akbar ended the jizya tax on non-Muslims, held debates in the Ibadat Khana and promoted Sulh-i-Kul, or universal peace.

Cultural developments

Painting workshops, Persian translations of Sanskrit texts and musicians like Tansen thrived at court.

Expansion

Conquests in Rajputana, Malwa, Gujarat, Bengal, Kashmir and Kabul made the empire a continental power.
